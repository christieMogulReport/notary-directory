Need something notarized in Columbus after work, on a Saturday, or on a Sunday? It comes up a lot (r/Columbus has a whole "Notary on Sunday" thread), and the usual answer, your bank, is closed by then. Below are the Columbus places with the latest posted evening and weekend notary hours, the free library option, plus mobile and online notaries for when everything is closed.

**Call ahead to confirm hours and notary availability.** A store being open doesn't guarantee a notary is in, so call the store first. Holiday hours change too: on the UPS Store pages we checked, Thanksgiving, November 26, is listed as closed (the Bethel Rd store lists noon–5 PM), November 27 as regular hours, Christmas Eve, December 24, as a short day ending at 3 PM, and Christmas Day, December 25, as closed. Hours below were copied from each business's own website on November 27, 2026. Bring a valid government-issued photo ID, and don't sign the document until you are in front of the notary.

## Quick picks

- **Sunday:** The UPS Store Olentangy Plaza (829 Bethel Rd) posts Sunday notary hours of 10 AM–6 PM. Graceland Blvd, N High St, East Main St and E Broad St post Sunday notary hours of 10 AM–3 PM. The Main Library is open Sundays 1–5 PM; library notary slots are by appointment and depend on staff availability, so check online before you count on a Sunday slot.
- **Weekday evenings:** The Main Library is open until 9 PM Monday through Thursday (free notary by appointment, basic documents only). Among UPS Stores, Bethel Rd, Graceland Blvd, East Main St, E Broad St, Stelzer Road and Westpointe Plaza post notary hours until 7 PM.
- **Saturday:** Bethel Rd posts Saturday notary hours until 6 PM; East Main St and E Broad St until 5 PM.
- **Late at night, or at home, a hospital or an office:** Horizon Mobile Notary says it is available 24/7, and Notary One says it can meet day or evening, 7 days a week, by appointment. Or use an online notary (below).
- **Cheapest:** The Columbus Metropolitan Library's notary service is free, by appointment, for basic documents (not deeds, wills, trusts, powers of attorney or real estate papers).

## When nothing is open: online notarization

Ohio law provides for online notaries public who notarize over audio-video communication (Ohio Revised Code 147.62 and 147.63: <https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-147.62>, <https://codes.ohio.gov/ohio-revised-code/section-147.63>). If you book a video session with a local notary, ask whether they are authorized as an Ohio online notary. The national services below work by video call on your own device and generally use notaries commissioned in other states. Many documents are accepted that way, but some offices, courts, title companies and foreign embassies may not accept an online notarization. Check with whoever will receive your document first.

- Proof (formerly Notarize): <https://www.proof.com/>
- BlueNotary: <https://bluenotary.us/>
- OneNotary: <https://onenotary.com/>
- NotaryLive: <https://notarylive.com/>

## Good to know in Ohio

If you're selling a car, Notary One's site points out that in Ohio the seller must personally appear and sign the title in front of the notary, so plan for the seller to be there. If you have an account at a bank or credit union, ask whether a notary is on staff; branch notaries usually work weekday hours only, and some branches open on Saturday mornings.
