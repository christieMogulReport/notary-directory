Need something notarized in Denver after work, on a Saturday, or on a Sunday? Below are Denver places that post notary hours on evenings and weekends, plus mobile notaries who come to you, including some who take calls around the clock.

**Call ahead to confirm hours and notary availability.** A store's posted notary hours depend on staff being in, and holiday hours change. Hours below were copied from each business's own website on November 20, 2026. Bring a valid government-issued photo ID, and don't sign the document until you are in front of the notary.

## Quick picks

- **Sunday:** Ten of the eleven Denver UPS Stores listed below post Sunday notary hours. The longest are The UPS Store Quebec Square (3700 Quebec St, 9 AM–5 PM) and The UPS Store W 38th Ave (2921 W 38th Ave, 10 AM–5 PM). The UPS Store S Broadway posts Sunday notary hours of 12–4:30 PM.
- **Weekday evenings:** The UPS Store W 38th Ave posts notary hours until 7:30 PM on weekdays, and Quebec Square posts until 7 PM. Most of the others post until 6:30 PM.
- **Saturday:** All eleven stores below post Saturday notary hours. Quebec Square posts the latest: until 6 PM.
- **Late night, at home, or at a hospital:** The mobile notaries below advertise evening and weekend appointments. Traveling Notary Denver posts hours of 7 AM–10 PM every day. Kristina Mobile Notary and Kennedy's Mobile Notary both advertise 24-hour service.

A UPS Store's notary hours can be shorter than its store hours. Each listing shows the notary hours the store posts. Two Denver UPS Store pages were left out: 3100 S Sheridan Blvd is marked "Coming Soon" and 6300 E Hampden Ave is marked "Permanently Closed."

## When nothing is open: online notarization

Colorado allows remote notarization by audio-video call, done by Colorado notaries who have been approved as remote notaries and who use a provider approved by the Colorado Secretary of State (Colorado Secretary of State remote notarization FAQ: <https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/notary/FAQ/remote.html>). Some documents can't be notarized this way. The FAQ says wills and records about the electoral process have special limits. Some receiving offices may also not accept an online notarization, so check first. These national services work by video call on your own device:

- Proof (formerly Notarize): <https://www.proof.com/>
- BlueNotary: <https://bluenotary.us/>
- OneNotary: <https://onenotary.com/>
- NotaryLive: <https://notarylive.com/>

## Check a notary's commission

You can look up a Colorado notary commission with the Colorado Secretary of State's "Verify a notary" search: <https://www.coloradosos.gov/notary/pages/public/verifyNotary.xhtml>. General notary information: <https://www.coloradosos.gov/pubs/notary/home.html>
